# Technical Debt

Use this skill to turn vague maintenance concerns into a prioritized, honest
debt assessment that supports a practical decision.

1. Describe the observed symptom, affected area, and current consequence.
2. Classify the debt as code, architecture, test, dependency, documentation, or
   infrastructure debt.
3. Separate confirmed evidence from hypotheses about root causes.
4. Identify who is affected: users, operators, developers, or the business.
5. Estimate impact in terms of defects, delay, cost, risk, or lost options.
6. Estimate likelihood and severity of harm if the debt remains.
7. Describe the effort, dependencies, and disruption required to address it.
8. Note whether the item compounds future work or blocks a planned change.
9. Compare remediation with containment, monitoring, acceptance, or deferral.
10. Prioritize high-impact, high-risk items whose effort is justified.
11. Make the prioritization rule explicit; impact and risk should outweigh a
    convenient but low-value cleanup.
12. Break approved remediation into small outcomes with observable completion
    criteria.
13. Record ownership, timing, and the cost of deferral.
14. Keep debt distinct from preference, speculative cleanup, and active defects.
15. Revisit priorities when requirements, risk, or evidence changes.

Do not label a concern as urgent debt without evidence of its impact or risk.
